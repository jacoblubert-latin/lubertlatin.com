  • Vocative = nominative, except 2nd-decl. -us → -e (Mārce) and -ius → -ī (fīlī).

  • 5th decl.: -ēī after a vowel (diēī), -eī after a consonant (reī).

STEP 4Ask what the case is doing

CaseCommon uses
Nominativesubject; predicate noun/adjective after est, sunt
Genitive“of”: possession, part of a whole (pars mīlitum), description
Dative“to / for”: indirect object; with special verbs (crēdō, pāreō, persuādeō) and certain adjectives
Accusativedirect object; place to which (ad, in + acc.); duration of time (trēs hōrās); subject of an indirect statement
Ablative“by, with, from, in, on”: means (gladiō), manner (cum cūrā), personal agent (ā/ab + abl.), time when (prīmā lūce), place where (in + abl.), separation, ablative absolute

Passive: the present, imperfect, and future use the same signs with passive endings (amābātur, dūcētur). The perfect system uses the perfect passive participle + sum: amātus est (perf.), erat (plupf.), erit (fut. perf.), sit (perf. subj.), esset (plupf. subj.).

Present-subjunctive vowels by conjugation: wE fEAr A lIAr. amEt · monEAt · dūcAt · capIAt · audIAt

STEP 5Watch for look-alikes

  • monet = present (2nd) · dūcet = future (3rd) · amet = present subjunctive (1st). Same -et, three different IDs. Find the conjugation first.
  • dūcam, audiam: future or present subjunctive (1st sg.). Context decides.
  • amāverit: future perfect or perfect subjunctive. Only the 1st sg. differs (amāverō / amāverim).
  • amāvēre on a perfect stem = amāvērunt, not an infinitive.
  • amāre: present infinitive or 2nd sg. present passive (= amāris).
  • -re- between stem and ending (dūceret) = imperfect subjunctive.
  • Vowel length can be the only clue: venit (comes) / vēnit (came); legit / lēgit.
  • sum at a glance: est, erat, erit, fuit, sit, esset.
